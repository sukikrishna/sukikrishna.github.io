Harvard University

I'm a PhD student at Harvard, co-advised by Finale Doshi-Velez and Maimuna Majumder. I also work with Reihaneh Rabbany and Jean-François Godbout in the Complex Data Lab at Mila.

I study how AI systems can support human judgment and decision-making in high-stakes settings, and what happens when they fail: when models hallucinate, are manipulated, or communicate confidence they shouldn't have, and how people respond. My work spans multi-agent AI safety (vulnerabilities and emergent behavior), interpretability, human-AI complementarity and oversight, epistemic integrity and fact-checking with LLMs, and AI for health.

Before Harvard, I earned a B.S. in Bioengineering from UC San Diego (valedictorian, summa cum laude) with minors in Data Science and Cognitive Science, where I did research in soft robotics and in machine learning for particle physics.

Outside the lab, I love trying new things, from figure skating and climbing to crochet, baking, and bird watching on the trail.

Current Research

What I'm working on and thinking about right now.

CollectiveScope: Detecting Emergent Behavior in Multi-Agent AI

Funded by a Cosmos Institute AI x Truth-Seeking grant (2026)

As AI systems increasingly act as groups of interacting agents, risky coordination can emerge that no single agent was designed for. CollectiveScope detects the onset of emergent collective behavior, identifies which agents drive it, and causally validates that attribution through targeted interventions.

INFERBench: Cross-System Vulnerability Reasoning

IBM Research, IT Automation team Mentors: Raju Pavuluri, Yu Deng, Rahul Krishna, Saurabh Jha

A benchmark testing whether AI agents can identify vulnerability chains that span realistic enterprise systems (application code, secrets, infrastructure-as-code, cloud/IAM, network policy, and agentic API trust) rather than a single vulnerable program.

AI Safety & Evaluation

Mechanistic Interpretability of ECG Foundation Models

Harvard SEAS

Do independently trained ECG foundation models learn shared, reproducible representations of the same clinical concepts, or do apparent "clinical features" depend on architecture, seed, dataset, and method? Using sparse autoencoders and causal tests, I also ask whether a clinical concept is partly a demographic one.

AI Safety & EvaluationCode

Epistemic Integrity & AI Fact-Checking

Harvard SEAS and Complex Data Lab, Mila Advisors: Reihaneh Rabbany, Jean-François Godbout, Finale Doshi-Velez

How do people respond to the confidence LLMs express? We find people recognize when a model's assertive language exceeds its actual confidence, yet still reward it, a tension we call confidence misalignment. I also contribute to Veracity, an open-source fact-checking assistant, and its browser extension, and study why fact-checking evaluation must separate ambiguous claims from genuine system failures.

AI for Health & Science

Epistemic Trespassing in COVID-19 Science

Harvard SEAS and Computational Health Informatics Program, Boston Children's Hospital Advisor: Maimuna Majumder

How did the rush of researchers from other fields reshape pandemic science? Across 450,000+ COVID-19 papers in OpenAlex, outbreak scientists never exceeded 12.5% of authors and 62.7% of papers had none on the team. Yet expert-led teams published in higher-impact venues and drew more citations, a pattern of expert dilution that fortified knowledge silos.

AI for Health & Science

Infrastructure-Aware AI Governance

Harvard T.H. Chan School of Public Health Advisor: Francesca Dominici

A quantitative framework for diversifiable risk in AI infrastructure investments, scoring site-level financial opacity, geographic vulnerability, resource stress, and community impact and aggregating them into portfolio-level exposure.
